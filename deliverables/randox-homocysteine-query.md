## Subject

Homocysteine (HOMO) via Home Collection Kit: pre-analytical handling query

## Body

Dear [rep name],

We order through Nexus as Veridian Clinic, a private GP practice, and include homocysteine (HOMO) in two of our panels.

With home collection and remote venous phlebotomy kits now available through Nexus, I can see that HOMO is listed as compatible with all three collection methods, including the Home Collection Kit, and it carries no special instructions on its panel details page.

I wanted to check how the pre-analytical requirement is handled, because homocysteine is unusually sensitive to it. Red cells continue to release homocysteine into plasma after venepuncture, so total plasma homocysteine rises with time at ambient temperature. The published figures are roughly 10 percent per hour where plasma is not separated within 30 minutes, and around 38 percent after six hours at room temperature. Samples kept on ice are stable for about three hours. The usual recommendation is separation within an hour, or transport on ice.

A postal sample will typically be in transit well beyond that window. My concern is not precision but bias: the drift is in one direction, so a result would read high rather than merely imprecise. Against a decision threshold of 15 micromol/L, and an optimisation target below 10, that is enough to turn a normal result into an apparently raised one.

Could you tell me:

1. Does the Home Collection Kit for homocysteine use a stabilised tube, a gel separator, a cold pack, or any other measure to control this?
2. Has Randox validated homocysteine specifically for home collection, and is there in-house stability data for the expected transit time?
3. Is there a maximum acceptable time from collection to receipt for this analyte, and is a sample rejected or flagged if it exceeds that?
4. Do homocysteine results from home collection carry a comment or a separate reference range?
5. I note Randox already prevents ordering of analytes considered unsuitable for self-collection, naming potassium and glucose. Has homocysteine been assessed against those criteria?

If home collection is not appropriate for this analyte, we will keep ordering it In Clinic, which is what we do today. I would rather understand the position than assume it.

You mention a list of markers not suitable for self-collection, and those on which Randox has performed full validation. Could you send both? It would let us design panels correctly rather than inferring from what Nexus allows.

Many thanks,

Dr Oluwatosin Taiwo
MBBS MRCGP MRCS

## Why it is worded this way

- **Leads with the ask, not the criticism.** Randox is the supplier and this is a question, not a complaint. If the kit already handles it, the answer is one line and we have lost nothing.
- **Cites the mechanism and the numbers.** A GP asking a laboratory a pre-analytical question gets a laboratory answer. A vague "is this reliable?" gets a sales answer.
- **Names the real risk as directional bias, not imprecision.** That is the point a lab will recognise immediately, and it is why this matters clinically rather than academically.
- **Uses Randox's own precedent.** They already exclude potassium and glucose from self-collection. Question 5 asks whether homocysteine was assessed on the same basis, which is difficult to answer with a brush-off.
- **Question 6 is the one with lasting value.** The validated-marker list stops us guessing at collection modes from Nexus icons for every future panel.

## Current position while we wait

No action needed. Both panels carrying homocysteine, Longevity and Metabolic Baseline, are **In Clinic only**, so every homocysteine we order today is drawn and processed conventionally. This only constrains a future postal panel.
