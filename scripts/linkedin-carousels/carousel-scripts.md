## Carousel 1: "7 Blood Tests Your GP Never Orders (But Should)"

**Cover slide:**
7 Blood Tests Your GP Never Orders
(And What They Would Tell You)
Dr Tosin Taiwo | GP | Veridian Clinic

**Slide 2: Fasting Insulin**
NHS checks your blood sugar.
Not your insulin.

Fasting insulin tells you HOW HARD your pancreas is working to keep that sugar normal.

Optimal: below 8 mIU/L
High-risk threshold: above 15 mIU/L

Most people with "normal" glucose have already been insulin-resistant for years.

**Slide 3: ApoB**
LDL cholesterol counts the lorries.
ApoB counts the loads.

A "normal" LDL with high ApoB means more artery-damaging particles per unit of cholesterol than the standard test shows.

ApoB is now the preferred cardiovascular risk marker in updated guidelines.
Your GP almost certainly has not tested it.

**Slide 4: Lipoprotein (a)**
Lp(a) is the cardiovascular risk factor you were born with.

It cannot be lowered by diet or exercise.
One in five people carry a significantly elevated level.
It is almost never tested on NHS panels.

If your parent had a heart attack before 60, you need this test.

**Slide 5: Homocysteine**
Homocysteine damages the lining of your blood vessels.

Elevated levels are linked to cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline and stroke.

It is easily treated with B vitamins.
It is almost never tested on a standard panel.

**Slide 6: hs-CRP**
Inflammation drives most of the chronic disease that kills people in the UK.

hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein) is the sensitive version of the CRP test your GP occasionally orders.

Standard CRP catches acute infection.
hs-CRP catches the low-grade chronic inflammation that precedes metabolic collapse.

**Slide 7: Free T3**
Your thyroid converts T4 to T3 to do its job.

NHS panels test TSH and sometimes T4.
Almost no NHS panel tests Free T3.

A patient with "normal" TSH and T4 can still have poor T3 conversion driving fatigue, weight gain and cold intolerance.

**Slide 8: HOMA-IR (from fasting insulin + glucose)**
HOMA-IR is not a test you order. It is a calculation from fasting insulin and fasting glucose.

It is the most accurate non-invasive assessment of insulin resistance available.

It requires fasting insulin to compute.
Your GP almost certainly has not tested your fasting insulin.

## Carousel 2: "Why Your Weight Won't Shift (A Clinical Framework)"

**Cover slide:**
Why Your Weight Won't Shift
The 5 Most Common Metabolic Blockers
Dr Tosin Taiwo | GP | Veridian Clinic

**Slide 2: Insulin Resistance**
If insulin is high, fat cells stay locked.

Insulin's job is to move glucose into cells.
When cells stop listening (insulin resistance), the pancreas pumps out more insulin.

High circulating insulin tells fat cells to store, not release.

You cannot out-exercise this.
You cannot out-willpower this.

You can measure it. Then you can fix it.

**Slide 3: Thyroid Dysfunction**
The NHS normal range for TSH is wide.
Very wide.

A TSH of 4.9 clears the lab normal.
Many patients feel and function significantly better with a TSH below 2.5.

Subclinical hypothyroidism suppresses resting metabolic rate.
It will not show up as "abnormal" on a standard test.

**Slide 4: Poor Sleep / Cortisol**
One night of poor sleep raises fasting insulin by 40%.

Cortisol released during chronic stress tells the liver to pump out more glucose.
That glucose triggers more insulin.
More insulin locks fat.

Treating the metabolic dysfunction without addressing sleep is building on sand.

**Slide 5: Gut and Liver Function**
A fatty liver dramatically reduces metabolic efficiency.

ALT and AST (liver enzymes) are included in NHS panels.
But they are usually only flagged when significantly elevated.

Early non-alcoholic fatty liver disease is present in 30% of UK adults.
It impairs glucose metabolism, hormone clearance and fat mobilisation.

**Slide 6: Hormonal Shift in Your 40s**
Testosterone falls in men from around 40.
Oestrogen declines in women from perimenopause.

Both hormones support muscle mass.
Muscle is metabolically active tissue.

Less muscle means lower metabolic rate at rest.
Most people replace muscle with fat over their 40s and do not realise the rate is compounding.

**Slide 7: The CGM**
A continuous glucose monitor shows your body's real-time response to food.

Not a fasting value. Not a 3-month average.
What actually happens after each meal. During stress. During sleep.

You can read a metabolic panel and still be operating blind without glucose pattern data.

## Carousel 3: "What 'Normal' Blood Tests Actually Mean"

**Cover slide:**
What "Normal" Actually Means on Your Blood Test
And Why It Is Not the Same as Optimal
Dr Tosin Taiwo | GP | Veridian Clinic

**Slide 2: How Reference Ranges Work**
NHS reference ranges are set at the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of a population.

"Normal" means: you are not in the bottom or top 2.5% of the tested population.

The tested population includes people who are unwell.
"Normal" does not mean "healthy."
"Normal" does not mean "optimal."

**Slide 3: Ferritin**
NHS normal: above 13 ug/L (women), above 30 ug/L (men)

Clinical optimal for energy: above 70 ug/L

A ferritin of 16 clears the lab normal for a woman.
She may still be exhausted due to functionally low iron stores.

She will be told: "Your bloods are normal."

**Slide 4: Testosterone (Men)**
NHS normal: above 8.7 nmol/L (most labs)

Clinical optimal for energy, mood, muscle: 15-30 nmol/L

A man with a testosterone of 9.2 is "normal."
He may have low libido, poor recovery, cognitive fog, and no energy.

He will be told: "Your bloods are normal."

**Slide 5: HbA1c**
NHS pre-diabetes range: 42-47 mmol/mol
NHS T2DM diagnosis: above 48 mmol/mol

Clinical concern threshold: above 39 mmol/mol

A patient with an HbA1c of 41 has no diagnosis.
But their fasting insulin may already be elevated. Insulin resistance may already be established.

They will be told: "Your bloods are normal."

**Slide 6: Free T3**
The NHS normal range for T4 is 12-22 pmol/L.

Many patients feel symptomatic and are functionally hypothyroid in the lower third of this range (12-15 pmol/L).

They will be told: "Your thyroid is fine."

**Slide 7: The Gap**
The gap between "not diseased" and "functioning well" is where private medicine operates.

This is not about chasing perfect numbers.
It is about finding the markers that explain how you feel and knowing what to do about them.

We test for optimal.
